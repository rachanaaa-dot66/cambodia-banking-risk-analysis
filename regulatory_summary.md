# Regulatory Framework Summary
## Cambodia Banking Sector Risk Management
### Basel III International Standards & NBC Local Regulations
**Prepared by:** Risk Analysis Team  
**Reference:** NBC Banking Supervision Reports | BIS Basel III Framework | NBC Prakas  
**Period:** Current as of 2024

---

## Executive Summary

Cambodia's banking sector operates under a dual regulatory framework —
international Basel III standards adopted and adapted by the National Bank
of Cambodia (NBC), and local Prakas (regulations) specific to Cambodia's
market context. Understanding this framework is essential for interpreting
the risk findings in this analysis, particularly the rising NPL ratios and
capital adequacy positions observed across the sector.

The key regulatory tension in Cambodia's current environment: NBC's
regulatory thresholds suggest the system is adequately capitalized (CAR
22.3% vs 15% minimum), but the rapid deterioration in asset quality
(NPL 2.0% → 7.2% in four years) indicates that current standards may
be insufficient to prevent the stress now emerging in individual
institutions.

## Part 1 — Basel III International Framework

### What is Basel III?

Basel III is the international regulatory standard for banks, developed
by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) at the Bank for
International Settlements (BIS) following the 2008 global financial
crisis. It sets minimum requirements for capital, liquidity, and
leverage that central banks globally — including NBC — use as the
foundation for local regulation.

### The Three Pillars Relevant to This Analysis

**Pillar 1 — Minimum Capital Requirements**

Banks must hold capital against three risk types:

| Risk Type | Minimum Capital | Cambodia Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Credit Risk | 8% of risk-weighted assets | Core issue — rising NPL |
| Market Risk | Based on VaR models | Relevant to bank trading books |
| Operational Risk | Standardized percentage | Process failures, fraud |

The total minimum Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) under Basel III is
**10.5%** including the capital conservation buffer. NBC sets Cambodia's
minimum at **15%** — more conservative than Basel III, reflecting the
higher risk environment of a frontier market.

**Key finding from our analysis:** Cambodia's system-wide CAR of 22.3%
appears comfortable. However, individual distressed banks likely have
CAR ratios approaching or breaching the 15% minimum as NPL losses
erode their capital base. NBC does not publish individual bank CAR
data publicly — this is a transparency gap worth noting.

**Pillar 2 — Supervisory Review**

Regulators (NBC) must assess whether banks hold sufficient capital
beyond the minimums, based on their specific risk profile. This
includes stress testing — modeling what happens to capital under
adverse scenarios.

**Relevance to Cambodia:** The rapid NPL deterioration from 5.2% to
7.2% in 2024 suggests NBC's Pillar 2 supervisory review should be
intensifying for the 8 banks identified as critical (NPL > 10%).
The scenario analysis in this project models exactly the kind of
stress scenarios Pillar 2 review requires.

**Pillar 3 — Market Discipline (Disclosure)**

Banks must publicly disclose sufficient information for market
participants to assess their risk profile.

**Relevance to Cambodia:** This is the weakest pillar in Cambodia's
current framework. Individual bank CAR ratios, detailed NPL
breakdowns by sector, and provisioning coverage ratios are not
consistently published. This opacity makes it harder for depositors,
investors, and analysts to identify distressed institutions early —
exactly the problem this project addresses using aggregated NBC data.

### Basel III Liquidity Requirements

Two ratios directly relevant to our liquidity risk findings:

**Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)**
```
LCR = High Quality Liquid Assets / Net Cash Outflows over 30 days
Minimum requirement: 100%
```
Ensures banks can survive 30 days of stress without external funding.
Banks with extreme loans-to-deposits ratios identified in our analysis
may struggle to meet LCR requirements if wholesale funding disappears.

**Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)**
```
NSFR = Available Stable Funding / Required Stable Funding
Minimum requirement: 100%
```
Ensures banks fund long-term assets with stable long-term liabilities.
Banks funding long-term real estate loans with short-term wholesale
borrowing violate the spirit of NSFR — a structural risk our
liquidity analysis identified.

### NPL Classification — Basel Standards vs Cambodia Practice

Basel guidelines suggest NPL classification after **90 days** of
non-payment. NBC follows this standard. However, during COVID-19,
NBC allowed loan restructuring that temporarily suspended NPL
classification — meaning the true NPL ratios in 2020–2021 were
likely higher than officially reported.

This regulatory forbearance explains the seemingly paradoxical data:
NPL ratio actually declined from 2.0% (2020) to 1.9% (2021) despite
the worst economic shock in decades. The stress was deferred, not
resolved — which is why NPL accelerated sharply from 2022 onward
as restructured loans matured.

## Part 2 — NBC Local Regulations (Prakas)

### What is a Prakas?

A Prakas is a formal regulatory instruction issued by NBC. It is the
primary instrument through which NBC implements both Basel standards
and Cambodia-specific requirements. Banks operating in Cambodia must
comply with all relevant Prakas or face regulatory sanctions.

### Key Prakas Relevant to This Analysis

**Prakas on Capital Adequacy**

NBC minimum CAR: **15%** (vs Basel III minimum of 10.5%)

The higher local minimum reflects:
- Cambodia's status as a frontier market with higher inherent risk
- Limited domestic capital markets to raise emergency capital
- Historical banking sector instability requiring larger buffers
- High dollarization creating additional currency risk exposure

**Impact of our findings:** Several distressed banks with NPL above
25–35% are almost certainly consuming capital rapidly as losses are
recognized. If CAR approaches 15%, NBC intervention becomes mandatory.

**Prakas on Loan Classification and Provisioning**

NBC classifies loans into five categories:

| Class | Days Past Due | Minimum Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 0–29 days | 1% |
| Special Mention | 30–59 days | 3% |
| Substandard | 60–89 days | 20% |
| Doubtful | 90–179 days | 50% |
| Loss | 180+ days | 100% |

**Critical implication:** A bank with 35% NPL (Hattha Bank) should
theoretically be provisioning 50–100% against those loans. If
provisioning is inadequate, reported capital is overstated. This is
why provision coverage ratio — data not publicly available from NBC
— is the missing piece in our analysis.

**Prakas on Large Exposure Limits**

NBC limits exposure to a single borrower or connected group at
**20% of bank capital**. This prevents concentration in individual
borrowers.

However, our sector analysis shows concentration at the portfolio
level — 22.7% of all banking sector loans in real estate — which
is not directly addressed by the single-borrower limit. A bank can
comply with large exposure limits while still being dangerously
concentrated in one sector.

**This is a regulatory gap** — Basel III addresses single-borrower
concentration but does not mandate specific sector concentration
limits. Cambodia's NBC has not yet issued sector-specific
concentration Prakas, though this would be a logical regulatory
development given current real estate exposure levels.

**Prakas on Liquidity**

NBC requires banks to maintain a minimum **liquidity ratio of 50%**
— liquid assets must be at least 50% of short-term liabilities.

This is less stringent than Basel III's LCR in some respects but
more prescriptive in others. Banks with high loans-to-deposits ratios
identified in our analysis may be managing close to this threshold
during stress periods.

## Part 3 — Regulatory Gaps and Emerging Issues

### Gap 1 — Insufficient Transparency on Individual Bank Health

NBC publishes aggregate sector data and individual bank NPL ratios
but does not consistently publish:
- Individual bank CAR ratios
- Provision coverage ratios by bank
- Deposit rate data by bank

This opacity prevented our analysis from fully assessing individual
bank resilience. Recommendation: NBC should move toward Basel III
Pillar 3 full disclosure standards for all licensed banks.

### Gap 2 — No Sector Concentration Limits

With 22.7% of total banking sector loans in real estate, Cambodia
has a systemic concentration risk that current regulations do not
directly address. A 20% decline in real estate values would create
losses far beyond what individual large-exposure limits capture.

Recommendation: NBC should consider issuing a Prakas setting
maximum sector concentration limits — for example 20% of total
loan book in any single sector — consistent with international
best practice.

### Gap 3 — Cross-Border Supervision of Subsidiaries

Many banks in Cambodia are wholly-owned subsidiaries of foreign
parents. NBC supervises the Cambodian entity but has limited
visibility into the parent's health and whether parent funding
is stable or at risk.

A parent bank in financial difficulty in Vietnam, Malaysia, or
Thailand could withdraw funding from its Cambodian subsidiary
with limited warning to NBC. This cross-border transmission
risk is not adequately addressed in current regulatory framework.

Recommendation: NBC should develop formal information-sharing
agreements with home country regulators of major foreign-owned
banks operating in Cambodia.

### Gap 4 — Forward-Looking Provisioning (IFRS 9)

Cambodia's provisioning rules are backward-looking — banks provision
after loans go bad. International standard (IFRS 9) requires
forward-looking Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provisioning — setting
aside reserves based on predicted future losses, not just current
observed losses.

Under IFRS 9, Cambodia's banking sector would likely show
significantly higher provisions today, reflecting the
deteriorating trajectory visible in our NPL trend analysis.

Recommendation: NBC should develop a roadmap for IFRS 9
adoption — likely a 3–5 year transition given current
capacity constraints.

## Part 4 — Regulatory Impact on This Analysis

### What Our Findings Mean in Regulatory Context

| Finding | Regulatory Implication |
|---|---|
| System NPL 7.2% above 5% threshold | NBC Pillar 2 review should intensify for all banks above 5% |
| 8 banks NPL > 10% | NBC mandatory intervention threshold likely triggered |
| Real estate concentration 22.7% | No current Prakas addresses this — regulatory gap |
| High LTD ratios at several banks | Liquidity Prakas compliance questionable for some institutions |
| Rapid NPL acceleration 2023–2024 | Suggests COVID restructuring masked true losses — forbearance effect unwinding |
| Parent-subsidiary funding dependency | Cross-border supervision gap — NBC limited visibility |

### The Regulatory Outlook

Based on regional precedent and Cambodia's current trajectory,
the following regulatory developments are likely within 2–5 years:

1. **Banking consolidation directive** — NBC likely to encourage or
   mandate mergers among distressed smaller institutions, following
   Thailand (1997) and Indonesia (1998) precedent

2. **Enhanced disclosure requirements** — pressure from IMF and
   World Bank to improve individual bank transparency

3. **Sector concentration limits** — real estate exposure likely
   to attract specific regulatory attention

4. **IFRS 9 adoption roadmap** — NBC has acknowledged the need
   for forward-looking provisioning standards

5. **Tighter licensing standards** — the number of banks (60+)
   is high for an economy of Cambodia's size; NBC likely to
   raise minimum capital requirements for new licenses and
   existing institutions

---

## Conclusion

Cambodia's regulatory framework provides a reasonable foundation
but contains meaningful gaps that have allowed the current stress
to develop without earlier intervention. The Basel III standards
NBC has adopted are sound, but implementation — particularly
around transparency, forward-looking provisioning, and sector
concentration — lags international best practice.

The risk findings in this analysis — rising NPL, concentration
in real estate, liquidity mismatches, parent-subsidiary
dependency — all correspond to identifiable regulatory gaps.
Addressing these gaps systematically would strengthen NBC's
ability to identify and respond to emerging stress earlier in
the cycle.
